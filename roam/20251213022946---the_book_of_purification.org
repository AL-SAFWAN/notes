#+title: The Book of Purification

* Chapter: Water

** Conditions of ṣalāh: purification

6. [@6] The _prayer_ has /conditions/ (shurūṭ, pl. of shart) that precede it, amongst them:
   - The most detailed condition of ṣalāh is purification.
   - There are 6 conditions related to purification.

7. Ritual purification (tahārah).
   - The Prophet ﷺ said: “Allah ﷻ does not accept a prayer without purification.” (Agreed upon)
     - Thus, the one who does not purify himself from:
       - ritual impurity (ḥadath), minor and major, and
       - physical impurities (najasah),
       renders his /prayer invalid/.

*** Tahārah

- *Tahārah*:
  - Linguistically:
    - purification/cleanliness.
  - Technically:
    - to remove the state of *ḥadath* (and what is similar to it), and
    - to remove *najasah* (physical impurity).

*** Ḥadath

- *Ḥadath*:
  - a *state* that a person enters into that prevents him from ṣalāh.
  - It is lifted by:
    - wuḍū’ (or tayammum when needed) for minor ḥadath,
    - ghusl (or tayammum when needed) for major ḥadath.
  - Example:
    - relieving oneself enters a person into ḥadath.

- Types of ḥadath:
  - Minor ḥadath:
    - lifted by wuḍū’.
  - Major ḥadath:
    - lifted by ghusl.

*** Najasah

- *Najasah*:
  - a *physical impurity* that must be removed.
  - Example:
    - human urine: it must be washed off.

*** Ḥadath vs najasah (important distinction)

- If a person relieves himself and does not wash the impurity:
  - then he performs wuḍū’:
    - his *ḥadath* is lifted,
    - but he still cannot pray because he still has *najasah* on him.
  - If he removes that *old* najasah (by washing it):
    - it does not break his wuḍū’,
    - because removing old najasah is not the same as entering a *new* state of ḥadath.

** Methods of purification (tahārah)

8. There are two methods of purification (tahārah):

9. The /first/ is purification by _water_, and this is the primary method of purification.
   - This is the default method.

** Water: what is purifying and what becomes impure

10. _All water_ that descends from the _sky_ or comes out from the _ground_ is purifying (tahūr).
    - Such water purifies:
      - from ritual impurity (ḥadath), and
      - from physical impurity (khabath/najasah),
      even if its /colour, taste, or smell/ changes due to something *pure*.

*** Proof (general principle)
- The Prophet ﷺ said:
  #+begin_quote
  “Verily water is pure, and nothing makes it impure.” (Abū Dāwūd & Tirmidhī)
  #+end_quote

*** Notes
- The default ruling of water is that it is *pure* and can be used for wuḍū’ and ghusl,
  until it is proven to be impure.
- If something pure changes the properties of the water, it still remains *tahūr* and can be used.
- However, if the change is so strong that it overcomes its name and properties (so it is no longer called “water”),
  then it cannot be used for wuḍū’.
  - Example: tea.

11. If one of its /three characteristics/ changes due to something /impure/, then it is considered /najis/ (impure).
    - Thus:
      - it is compulsory to keep away from it, and
      - one cannot use it for purification.
    - Example:
      - a dog urinating in a small amount of water (so the water’s properties are overcome),
        as opposed to a large body like the ocean where the properties are not overcome.

** Principles: default purity and certainty vs doubt

12. The _original ruling_ of things is that they are _pure_ and _permissible_ to use.

13. Thus, if one /doubts/ the purity of water, clothes, a surface, or anything else, it is considered _ṭāhir_ (pure).

*** Example (the opposite case—certainty of ḥadath)
- If a person is certain he entered ḥadath (e.g. he went to the toilet),
  but he doubts whether he made wuḍū’,
  then the certainty is that he *does not* have wuḍū’.

*** Remaining upon purity when doubting a nullifier
- Likewise, if one was sure that he was initially in a state of purification
  and then /doubted/ whether he broke his purification,
  he remains _ṭāhir_ due to the statement of the Prophet ﷺ
  to the person who imagined he had passed wind during prayer:

  #+begin_quote
  “One should not leave (his ṣalāh) until he hears a sound or finds a smell.” (Agreed upon)
  #+end_quote

*** Principle
- certainty is not removed by doubt.
- you ignore the doubt and stick with what is certain.

* Chapter: Utensils
** General Principles

14. [@14] All utensils are _permissible_.
    - this also includes containers, since we store water in containers.

** Exception: gold and silver

15. Except /gold and silver/ utensils, and that which contains them,
    - _except_ for a small amount of silver due to a pressing need (ḥājah),
      - with the condition that you cannot repair it with anything else.

- This is due to the statement of the Prophet ﷺ:

#+begin_quote
“Do not drink from containers made of gold and silver and do not eat from its (gold and silver) bowls, for verily it is for them in this world and for us in the Afterlife.” (Agreed upon)
#+end_quote

*** Warnings and explanation
- whoever drinks from a silver vessel, the Fire of Jahannam boils in his stomach.
- the reason is that it is for us in the Hereafter and not the dunyā,
  - and the reason is not due to its price.
    - hence it would be permissible for a wealthy person to have an expensive container,
      - however, for someone who does not have wealth, then it will still be prohibited.

*** Scope of the prohibition
- the main use of containers is for eating and drinking,
  - so lesser uses (like making wuḍū’ from them) are also prohibited.
         
* Chapter: Istinjā’ and the Etiquettes of Relieving Oneself

** Definition
- istinjā’ is purifying yourself after relieving oneself
  - it can be with water or a solid material
    - or just water

** Entering the place of relieving oneself

16. It is recommended that if one enters an area to relieve oneself, that one enters with the left foot (before the right) and says:

*** Entering with the left foot
- scholars have derived that:
  - if you are going from a better place to a worse place, you enter with your left foot
  - if you are going from a worse place to a better place, you enter with your right foot

*** Duʿā’ when entering
#+begin_quote
Bismillah, Allahumma inni a‘udhu bika minal khubthi/khubuthi wal khaba’ith
#+end_quote

- khubthi:
  - all evil and evil spirits
- khubuthi:
  - male and female shayāṭīn
- Bismillah:
  - not authentically established

#+begin_quote
“In the name of Allah, O Allah! I seek refuge in You from male & female devils.”
#+end_quote

** Leaving the place of relieving oneself

17. And when one leaves, one should:

   1. leave with the right foot
   2. say:

#+begin_quote
Ghufranaka
“O Allah, I seek Your forgiveness.”
#+end_quote

#+begin_quote
al hamdulillahil-ladhi adh-haba ‘annil atha wa ‘afani
“Praise is to Allah ﷻ Who has relieved me of impurity and given me good health.”
#+end_quote

- this is not authentically established

** Etiquettes while relieving oneself

18. One should support themselves by putting more weight on their left leg and erecting their right.
- if it is medically proven to be beneficial, then it will be recommended; otherwise it is mubāḥ

19. One should conceal themselves with a barrier or by something else.

20. One should distance themselves from people if one is in an open area.
- you should not relieve yourself in front of people

21. It is not permissible to answer the call of nature in:
- pathways,
- places where people sit,
- underneath trees that give fruit,
- or in an area that will harm other people.

22. It is not permitted to face the qiblah or turn one’s back to it while relieving oneself, due to the saying of the Prophet ﷺ:

#+begin_quote
“If any one of you goes to an open space for answering the call of nature, he should neither face nor turn his back towards the qiblah while urinating or defecating. Instead, he should face the east or west.” (Agreed upon)
#+end_quote

** Cleaning after relieving oneself

23. After relieving oneself, one should:

   1. Do _Istijmār_ (cleaning the private parts using a solid material) with _three stones_ or something similar that cleans the required area.
      - if you use tissue first, you will not touch the najāsah directly

   2. Then one should do _istinjā’_ (cleaning the private parts with water until the impurity is washed away)

24. It is sufficient to restrict oneself to _one of the two_ (i.e. istijmār or istinjā’).

** Conditions and restrictions of istijmār

25. One /cannot/ perform _istijmār_ with the following:

*** Conditions of istijmār
- use something that is pure, and that the thing you are using cleans with
  - impurity does not cling to it
- wipe a minimum of 3 times
  - there are no traces of impurity on the final wipe

*** What cannot be used
1. /Dung or bones/, as that was prohibited by the Prophet ﷺ.
2. Anything of sanctity,
   - or great value.

* Chapter: The Removal of Impurities and an Account of Impure Substances
** General rule: removing impurities

26. [@26] It is sufficient to _wash_ all impurities—whether they are on the body, clothes, the area (one prays in), or any other area—by removing the impurity itself from the area.
    - This is because the *Legislator did not stipulate* for the washing of all impurities a specific number of washes,
    - /except/ in the case of the impurity of a dog, since the Legislator stipulated washing the impurity of the dog seven washes, *one with earth*, as in the ḥadīth which is agreed upon.

** Impure substances (najāsāt)

27. The following things are impure:

*** 1. Human urine
- Human urine.

*** 2. Human faeces
- Human faeces.

*** 3. Blood
- Blood,
  - /except/ for a small amount, as that is excused.
  - An example (of impure blood) is blood that has been spilt from slaughtering animals that are permissible to eat,
    - /except/ for the blood that remains in the meat and veins, for that is pure.
  - this is according to the vast majority, and consensus is also quoted.
    - however, the evidence does not have a clear-cut backing to this,
      - as we find evidence for the opposing view.
      - so on the side of caution, and not going against the majority,
        - it is safer to wash the blood away.
    - some say that the consensus was based on menstruation blood.

*** 4. Urine and faeces of animals that are impermissible to eat
- The urine and faeces of every animal that is impermissible to eat,
  - e.g. dogs, donkeys, lions, cats.

*** 5. Predators
- All predators.

*** 6. Dead animals not slaughtered correctly
- Likewise, /all dead/ animals that were not slaughtered according to ritual requirements,
  - /except/ for:
    - dead humans, and
    - that which does not have flowing blood (e.g. flies, mosquitoes), and
    - fish and locust, since they are pure.

- Impurity transfers through things that are moist, or when you can visibly see it,
  - but not when it is dry.

Allah ﷻ says in the Qur’an:

#+begin_quote
“You are forbidden to eat carrion; blood; pig’s meat; any animal over which any name other than God's has been invoked; any animal strangled, or victim of a violent blow or a fall, or gored or savaged by a beast of prey, unless you still slaughter it [in the correct manner]; or anything sacrificed on idolatrous altars...” (5:3)
#+end_quote

The Prophet ﷺ said:

#+begin_quote
“The believer does not become impure while alive or dead.” (Agreed upon)
#+end_quote

He ﷺ also said:

#+begin_quote
“Two types of carrion and blood have been made permissible. As for the two types of carrion, (they are) fish and locust. As for the two types of blood, (they are blood from) the liver and spleen.” (Ahmad and Ibn Mājah)
#+end_quote

** Pure substances and special rulings

*** Urine and faeces of edible animals
28. The /faeces and urine/ of animals that are _permissible_ to eat are _pure_.
    - e.g. sheep, camels, cows, and goats.
    - e.g. if bird droppings land on your garment and you pray in it, your prayer is still valid.

*** Maniyy (semen)
29. Maniyy (semen) of a human is pure.
    - The Prophet ﷺ used to wash it from his clothes when it was moist,
      - and would scrape it off his clothes when it was dry.

*** Urine of a baby boy
30. It is sufficient to _sprinkle water_ on the urine of a *baby boy* that does not eat solid food out of desire for it, since the Prophet ﷺ said:

#+begin_quote
“The urine of a baby girl is to be washed and the urine of a baby boy is to be sprinkled over.” (Abū Dāwūd and Nasā’ī)
#+end_quote

- the urine is a lighter form of impurity,
  - it is between purity and impurity,
    - based on the texture of urine due to solid food.

** Removing an impure substance
31. If the /impure/ substance is _removed_ from the affected area, then the area is _pure_.
    - Any residual colour or smell does not affect the ruling.
      - This is due to the statement of the Prophet ﷺ to Khawlah رضي الله عنها regarding the blood of menstruation:

#+begin_quote
“Water suffices you and its effects (from the blood) will not harm you.” (Abū Dāwūd & Ahmad)
#+end_quote

- these are things that must be removed in order for our prayer to be valid.

* Chapter: The Description of Ablution
** Definition of wuḍū’
- linguistically:
  - that which shines
- technically:
  - to wash and wipe certain limbs in a certain manner as an act of worship

** The most complete description of wuḍū’

32. [@32] The ablution is performed by:

*** 1. Intention (niyyah)
1. Intending the removal of /ḥadath/ (ritual impurity), or intending ablution for prayer, etc.
   - or any intention related to an act of ʿibādah

- the intention is a /sharṭ/ (condition) for all actions of purification and other than it.
  - every act of worship requires intention
    - every deed requires intention, and there are two intentions for it:
      1. the deed itself
      2. for the sake of Allah
    - if you know what you are doing, then that means you have intended it
      - as long as you know what you are doing, you do not need to pronounce it
        - intention is in the mind and the heart

*** 2. Saying “Bismillāh”
2. Then to say: Bismillāh
   - all the narrations about this are weak and not authentically attributed
     - hence it is not masnūn to say this
       - we have ḥadīth related to other companions describing the wuḍū’,
         - but they did not start with “Bismillāh”
   - the ruling for men and women is the same, unless it is specified

*** 3. Washing the hands
3. Then to wash the hands until the wrists three times.
   - from the tips of the fingers to the wrists
     - this is sunnah
       - this is recommended

*** 4. Madmaḍah and istinshāq
4. Then to do madmaḍah (taking water into the mouth and then expelling it) and istinshāq (taking water into the nose and then expelling it) three times with three handfuls of water.
   - put water into your hand, then with that same handful:
     - put water in the mouth, then in the nose
       - circulate the water in the mouth to the back of the molars
       - in the nose: inhale, then blow it out, removing any mucus
   - this is doing it once
     - found in the ḥadīth of ‘Abdullāh ibn Zayd
   - as for doing it six times (handfuls)…

- the majority say:
  - this is a sunnah act of wuḍū’ and not wājib
    - what Allah has commanded is in the āyah, and other than that is recommended

*** 5. Washing the face
5. Then to wash the face three times.
   - the boundaries of the face are:
     - from the original hairline to the bottom of the chin
     - and from ear to ear (but does not include the ears)
       - e.g. the boundary between the ear and the beard
   - if you have a thin beard (so the skin can be seen):
     - you should put your fingers through it so the water reaches the skin
   - if you have a thick beard (so the skin cannot be seen):
     - it is sufficient to wash over it

- what is obligatory is to wash the face once
  - the sunnah is to wash it three times
  - and some scholars say it is prohibited to wash more than this

*** 6. Washing the arms
6. Then to wash the arms until the elbows.
   - from the tips of the fingers
     - to the end of the elbows, including the elbows
   - wash the right side first, then the left side
   - washing means the water trickles over the limbs
     - not wiping the limbs with water

*** 7. Wiping the head
7. Then, to wipe the entire head with both hands once:
   - starting from the beginning of the head up until the nape,
     - and then returning back to the beginning of the head
   - this is to be done one time
   - from wet hands (wiping, not washing)
   - wipe from the front to the back (to the nape, not the neck) and then back to the front
   - if you have long hair:
     - you do not need to wipe what extends beyond the head
     - if you wipe the hair in any direction, it is sufficient
   - there is nothing authentic about wiping the neck

*** 8. Wiping the ears
8. Then to insert one’s index fingers into the ears and to wipe the outer part of the ear with the thumb.
   - the index finger goes into the ear
   - the thumb is used for the back of the ear

*** 9. Washing the feet
9. Then to wash the feet (including the ankles) three times each.
   - from the tips of the toes to the ankles, including the heels

- This is the most complete way of performing the ablution.

** Other sunnah acts after wuḍū’

*** Dhikr after wuḍū’
- say:

Muslim (234) narrated from ‘Umar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb (may Allah be pleased with him) that the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings of Allah be upon him) said:

but the eight gates of Paradise will be opened for him and he will enter through whichever one he wants.”

Narrated by Muslim, 234.
#+end_quote

was narrated by al-Nasā’ī in ‘Amal al-Yawm wal-Laylah, and by al-Ḥākim in al-Mustadrak, from Abū Sa‘īd al-Khudrī (may Allah be pleased with him). #+end_quote

*** Two rakʿahs after wuḍū’
- perform 2 rakʿahs (sunnah) of wuḍū’ with complete concentration
  - it is better to keep the ṣalāh short
    - so we can completely focus on the ṣalāh

** Obligatory parts of wuḍū’ (farā’iḍ)

33. The obligatory parts of the ablution are:

*** 1. Washing/wiping each limb once
1. To wash (each limb) once.
   - there are 4:
     - face — wash
     - arms — wash
     - head — wipe
     - feet — wash

*** 2. Order (tartīb)
2. To perform the actions of ablution in the order (tartīb) described in the verse:

#+begin_quote
“O you who believe! When you rise to perform prayer, wash your faces and your forearms to the elbows and wipe over your heads and wash your feet to the ankles...” (5:6)
#+end_quote

- this is only for the farḍ once, not the sunnah repetitions

*** 3. Succession (muwālāt)
3. To wash each limb in succession, such that there isn’t a customary long gap between the washing of different limbs.
   - the “long gap” is according to the custom of the people
   - this holds true for everything that stipulates muwālāt (matters to be done consecutively without a long gap)

* Chapter: Wiping of Footwear and Bandages
** 34. Wiping over khuffs and what resembles them

*** Scope: not limited to leather
34. If one is wearing two khuffs or something similar to them, he can wipe over them if he wishes:
- wiping over socks is not limited to leather socks
  - it includes anything similar to them,
    - i.e. similar in a way that is known by the people
      - Imām al-Nawawī said it is what is known to keep the feet warm

*** Conditions related to socks
- the socks:
  - must be made from something pure
  - the original socks must cover the place of obligation (what is obligatory to wash)
    - the sock may be worn out with holes
      - you can still wipe over them
        - if they cling to the feet and are still known as “socks” by the people
    - ankle socks cannot be used, since they do not cover what is obligatory to wash from the feet
      - they can only be used in wuḍū’ that includes washing the feet

*** Taking socks off and putting them back on
- taking the socks off does not invalidate the wuḍū’
  - e.g. Ibn Ḥazm said it is like a person who does wuḍū’ and then shaves off his head
- but putting them back on:
  - you cannot wipe over them again,
    - since it came from a wuḍū’ based on wiping, not washing
      - and the time of wiping is defined by the first wipe after washing the feet

*** Wearing socks on top of socks
- wearing socks on top of socks:

  1. you wear the socks on before you break the wuḍū’
     - you can choose either pair, and the wuḍū’ is valid

  2. you wear the second pair after breaking wuḍū’, when you did not wipe over the first pair (before wiping the first pair)
     - you cannot wipe over the second pair, as it was not worn with ṭahārah
       - you have to wipe over the first pair

  3. you wear the second pair after breaking wuḍū’, when you wiped over the first pair
     - there is khilāf on this
       - if you put it on when you are pure:
         - the condition of wiping is based on timing,
           - so the timing is according to the first pair
       - if you put on two pairs and wipe over the top pair, then break wuḍū’:
         - can you wipe over the top pair?
           - yes, since the bottom pair was always there from the beginning
       - if you wear socks, then take them off and wear another pair:
         - you can wipe over them,
           - since it is a wuḍū’ that is based on having washed your feet

*** Is it better to wash the feet or wipe?
- is it better to wash the feet or wipe over the socks?
  - it is better to wipe, since you are taking the easier of the two

*** How to wipe over them
- how do we wipe over them?
  - wet the hand and wipe from the tips up to the shin above the ankle level at the same time (more preferred)
    - wipe the right foot once and the left foot once

*** Duration and conditions
1. For one day and night if one is a muqīm (resident), and for three days and three nights if one is a musāfir (traveller).
   - it begins from when he wipes for the first time
     - the 24-hour limit is the limit of wiping, not of wuḍū’
       - so the limit can end, but your wuḍū’ is still valid

2. With the condition that he wore the footwear while in a state of ṭahārah (purification).

3. One can only wipe over them if in a state of minor ritual impurity (al-ḥadath al-aṣghar).
   - not for major impurity

#+begin_quote
It was reported on the authority of Anas رضي الله عنه that the Prophet ﷺ said:
“If one of you performs ablution and puts on his footwear, let him wipe over them and pray in them, and he doesn’t have to remove them except due to janābah (i.e. sexual impurity).”
(al-Ḥākim)
#+end_quote

** 35. Wiping over a bandage or medicine

35. If one has a bandage or medicine on a limb that is usually washed during ablution, and washing it would be harmful, then one can wipe over it for both minor and major ritual impurity until the wound has healed.

*** Conditions and related notes
- with the condition that the cast covers that which is required
  - e.g. putting a cast on the arm only at the wrist, which would not cover what is required

- what to do when it comes to the point where you cannot even wash or wipe:
  - some say to do tayammum
  - others say to do wuḍū’ and skip that part
    - however, you should do tayammum for that part, since you could not do wuḍū’ for it
      - and the replacement of wuḍū’ is tayammum, not abandonment

** 36. Method of wiping over footwear

36. The method of wiping over footwear is to wipe most of its upper part.

** 37. Wiping over the jabīrah (bandage/cast)

37. As for the jabīrah (bandage/cast), then one should wipe over all of it.

*** Time limit and scope
- wiping over the bandage has no time limit
  - you can wipe over it for major and minor ḥadath
- wuḍū’ is not a condition to wipe over it
  - the bandage can be on any body part

** 40. [@40] The manner of ghusl from janābah
As for the method by which the Prophet ﷺ performed ghusl from janābah:

*** 1. Washing the private parts first
1. He would wash his private part first.
   - making sure nothing from intimacy remains there
     - so he (ﷺ) would pour water onto his hand and wash
       - and then strike the floor or the wall

*** 3. Pouring water over the head three times
3. Then he would pour water on his head three times, making sure that the water reached his scalp.
   - he (ﷺ) would run his fingers into his hair to make sure it reached the scalp

*** 4. Pouring water over the rest of the body
4. Then he would pour water over the rest of his body.

*** 5. Washing the feet in another area
5. Then he would wash his feet in another area.

**** Combining the narrations
- the ḥadīth of Maymūnah shows that he (ﷺ) washed his feet in a different place
- the ḥadīth of ʿĀ’ishah shows that he did wuḍū’ and did not wash his feet (at that moment)
  - the context is that if there is dirt on the floor, it would get on the feet, hence he (ﷺ) would wash them after
  - combining them:
    - if there is no water (collecting) from the bath, then we can wash the feet during wuḍū’
    - and if there is water there, then we can wash them at the end

** Rulings related to bleeding

*** 49. Default ruling: treat the blood as menstruation
49. [@49] Blood that is discharged from a woman's private part should at first be considered to be menstrual blood, regardless of:
- age,
- amount of blood, or
- repetition.

**** Distinguishing between two types of blood
- menstrual blood has its characteristics
- there are two types of blood:
  - menstrual blood, or
  - blood from a vein

- how to differentiate:
  - by colour:
    - menstrual blood is darker
    - vein blood is lighter
  - by thickness:
    - menstrual blood is thicker
    - vein blood is thinner
  - by smell:
    - menstrual blood has a smell
    - vein blood does not
  - by clotting:
    - menstrual blood does not clot (as it has already occurred)
    - vein blood clots after it leaves

**** the reason behind the ruling
- the blood of menstruation is harmful
  - hence, if this blood is present, then it is harmful
    - the ruling revolves around its reason

*** 50. Continuous bleeding: mustaḥāḍah
50. [@50] Except if the blood continuously flows and does not stop except for a short while.
- in that case she will be considered to be *mustaḥāḍah* (i.e. suffering from defective bleeding)

**** Why this is a problematic ruling
- who defines what “a short while” is?
  - some say 10 days
  - some say one-third of a month
    - not half a month
      - there is no clear text for this
        - but it requires a boundary so that we can say this is no longer menses

- the reason it becomes a problem:
  - if a woman is bleeding for 25 days,
    - then it would mean she can only pray for 3–4 days
      - and that would mean she would need to make up prayers (and this would continue throughout the year)

- hence they exercise their ijtihād:
  - e.g. one-third is mentioned in inheritance and the Prophet (ﷺ) said this is a lot
  - others say a half
  - others say the default ruling:
    - she does not pray until it stops for a period

*** 51. Returning to the usual cycle (‘ādah)
51. [@51] The Prophet ﷺ commanded the mustaḥāḍah to observe the rulings of menstruation according to the duration of her usual cycle.

*** 52. If she has no usual cycle: distinguish the blood
52. [@52] If she doesn’t have a regular cycle (‘ādah), then she has to try and distinguish between:
- the blood of menstruation, and
- the blood that results from a defective condition.

- e.g. if the same pattern occurs for 3–4 months, this becomes her ‘ādah
  - this is what she returns to
- if she does not have a pattern:
  - she needs to try to differentiate the blood

*** 53. If she cannot distinguish: six or seven days
53. [@53] If she is unable to distinguish between the types of blood, she should then consider her menstruation to be six or seven days, as that is the average for women.
- the general norm of her family first
