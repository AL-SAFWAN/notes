******* The Kaʿbah and its foundations
Allah decreed for *Ibrahim and his son Ismail to raise the foundations of the Kaʿbah*.
Some tafsir mention that the original foundations existed before Ibrahim, either, from Ādam or by the angels — and that Ibrahim rebuilt it upon these foundations.

******* The Arabs and the Hajj before Islam
Although Quraysh and the Arab tribes committed shirk, they still *preserved* many rituals of Ibrahim, including:
- ṭawaf around the Kaʿbah
- honouring of the sacred months
- serving the pilgrims
- visiting Makkah during the months of Hajj

During the Hajj season, tribes from all over Arabia would come to Makkah, and the merchants of Quraysh would *raise their prices*.
This made Quraysh *wealthy* and gave them *prestige* as servants of the House of Allah.

******* Abraha’s envy
There was a man from Yemen named *Abraha al-Ashram*, the Abyssinian governor under the Christian kingdom.
He became jealous of the honour and wealth Quraysh gained during the pilgrimage season.

He gathered the Christians and said:
 #+begin_quote
  I will build for you a magnificent church so that people from all regions come to visit it instead of the Kaʿbah.
 #+end_quote

So he constructed a massive cathedral in sana, called al-Qullays, the tallest and most decorated building in Arabia at that time.
He announced that he intended to divert the Arab pilgrimage to his church, claiming:
 #+begin_quote
  Are we not the true followers of Ibrāhīm and of ʿisa?
  Why should they make pilgrimage to the land of idol-worshippers?
 #+end_quote

He wrote to the surrounding kings instructing them to send their pilgrims to this church.
He also publicly declared his intention to destroy the Kaʿbah.

******* The Arabs respond
When the Arab tribes heard what Abraha planned, they were enraged.
A man from the tribe of Kinanah, named Ibn Nadir (mentioned in early sirah accounts), travelled to al-Qullays and defecated inside the church, showing contempt for Abraha’s attempt to replace the Kaʿbah.

When Abraha heard this, he said:
 #+begin_quote
  How dare they defile this sacred place!
  I will march against them with an army and destroy their House.
 #+end_quote
He began preparing an army, including war elephants.

******* Abraha assembles his army
Abraha sought assistance from Christian kingdoms across the region.
They sent soldiers, weapons, and support.
Abraha marched with a huge army accompanied by elephants, the most famous of which in narrations is Mahmud, his main elephant.

******* Passing through the Arabian tribes
Abraha reached a region called Khathʿam.

Their leader, Nufayl ibn Habib al-Khathʿami, tried to mobilise his people to resist, but after seeing the size of the army, he realised they could not fight.

He was captured and initially sentenced to death by the yemeni army, but Abraha spared him and kept him as a guide, forcing him to show the army the route toward Makkah.

******* Passing Ta’if
When the army reached Ta’if, the tribe of Thaqif came out to meet Abraha.
They had their own temple, al-Lāt, and they did not respect the Kaʿbah as Quraysh did.
They told Abraha:
 #+begin_quote
  “We are with you.
  We hear and we obey.
  We have no attachment to the Kaʿbah.”
 #+end_quote

One of their men, Abu Righal, guided Abraha’s army to the borders of Makkah, to Mughammas and died there.

The Arabs despised him for helping the destroyer of the Kaʿbah, and classical narrations mention that his grave was stoned for generations as a sign of hatred.

The Arabs hated him for betraying the sanctuary, the made it in a tradition to used to stone his grave, after hajj.
                
******* Abraha in Mughammas
Abraha sent a group of men from al-Ḥabasha (Abyssinia) ahead of his army to enter Makkah and loot its wealth.

They returned with many valuables, including two hundred camels belonging to ʿAbd al-Muttalib, the grandfather of the Prophet Muḥammad (ﷺ).

'Abd al-Muttalib

- extremely generous and noble
- deeply respected by all the Arabs
- the leader of Quraysh and the leader of the people of Makkah
- the one entrusted with the keys of the Kaʿbah
- What he decided was treated as final and binding for the people of Makkah.
******* 'Abd al-Muttalib goes to Abraha
Then he heard that his camels had been taken, ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib went to the location of Abraha’s camp.
He was shown Abraha’s large tent and requested permission to enter.

Abraha allowed him in, and they sat together with a translator between them.

ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib said:
  #+begin_quote
   I want you to return my two hundred camels.
  #+end_quote
Abraha said, surprised:
  #+begin_quote
  I am coming to destroy your House (the Kaʿbah) and your religion,
  and you are only talking to me about your camels?
  #+end_quote
ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib replied:
  #+begin_quote
  I am the owner of the camels,
  and this House has a Lord who will protect it.
  #+end_quote
Abraha then told him to leave.
ʿAbd al-Muttalib remained firm and insisted he would not leave except on the condition that his camels were returned to him.
So they returned the camels to him.
        
******* Making dua at the kabah
Straight after ʿAbd al-Muttalib went directly to the kabah.

Then he pleading to Allah holding the ropes of the kabah, saying:
 #+begin_quote
  O Allah, free Quraysh (from this threat).
  We are only following the sunnah of Ibrahim in honouring this House.
  This is the House which Ibrahim built.
 #+end_quote
 
Then he called all of Quraysh and ordered them to leave Makkah and withdraw to the surrounding hills and high places, and wait for the punishment of Allah to appear and for Allah to protect His House.
 
******* The elephants refuse to approach the Kaʿbah
The army then tried to march forward with the elephants.

Before they moved, Nufayl ibn Habib al-Khathʿami  came close to the main elephant and said:

But when they tried to make it walk towards the Kaʿbah, it refused to move and would kneel and stay fixed to the ground.

***** Just before Fajr

Many early sources report a miraculous light associated with the Prophet’s birth.

In Musnad Ahmad, from al-‘Irbad ibn Sāriyah, the Prophet ﷺ said his mother saw at his delivery a light that illuminated the palaces of Syria; Ibn Hibban and al-Hakim graded it Sahih, and al-Haythami called its chain ḥasan. Other narrations (Ibn Hishām, al-Ṭabarī, al-Ṭabarānī) describe a dream-vision during pregnancy and a light that filled the house at birth. Ibn Kathīr reconciles them by saying she dreamed it while pregnant and then saw it realized with her eyes at delivery

Ibn Hajar said in Fat'h Al-Baari:

“Among the signs of his Prophecy that appeared at his birth (ﷺ) and after his birth was what At-Tabaraani narrated from 'Uthmaan ibn Abi Al-‘Aas Ath-Thaqafi, from his mother, that she went to Aaminah, the mother of the Prophet (ﷺ) and she related that when she [Aaminah] was in labor (i.e. giving birth), ‘I was looking at the stars getting closer to me to the point that I thought they would fall on me; and when she gave birth, a light came out of her that lit the whole house.’”

This hadeeth is supported by another hadeeth narrated by Al-Irbaadh ibn Saariyah, who said:

“I heard the Prophet (ﷺ)  say, ‘(Allah has written in the Preserved book) that I am the Slave of Allah and the Seal of the Prophets while Adam was not yet created. I will inform you about that: I am (the result of) the supplication of my father Ibraaheem (Abraham), the glad tiding given about me by ‘Eesa (Jesus), and the vision which my mother saw – like what all the mothers of Prophets see – and that when the mother of the Prophet (ﷺ) gave birth to him, she saw a light that lit up the palaces of the Levant.” [Ahmad - Ibn Hibbaan and Al-Haakim graded it saheeh (sound)]

****** Salmān al-Fārisī (رضي الله عنه) — Journey to Islām (Narration)
******* 1. Early life in Persia (Isfahān → village of Jayy)
- Salmān said:
  - He was a Persian man from Isfahān, from a village called Jayy.
  - His father was the chieftain of the village.
  - His father loved him intensely and kept him confined at home (like girls were kept in).
  - He was raised upon the religion of the Majūs (fire-worship).
  - He strove hard until he became the keeper of the fire.
  - He tended it and did not let it go out.

******* 2. The garden errand and first encounter with Christianity
- His father owned a large garden.
- One day his father was busy with construction and sent him:
  - To check the garden and complete some tasks.
- On the way:
  - He passed by a Christian church.
  - He heard their voices in prayer.
  - He entered to see what they were doing.
  - He was impressed by their worship and drawn to their way.
  - He said to himself that this was better than their religion.
  - He stayed until sunset and forgot his father’s garden errand.

******* 3. Confrontation with his father and being restrained
- He returned late.
- His father had already sent people to search for him.
- His father questioned him for neglecting the garden work.
- Salmān told his father:
  - He saw people praying in a church.
  - He was impressed by their religion and stayed until sunset.
- His father responded:
  - There is nothing good in that religion.
  - Your religion and the religion of your forefathers is better.
- Salmān insisted:
  - Their religion was better.
- His father feared for him:
  - Chained his legs (fetters).
  - Confined him in the house.

******* 4. Secret plan to escape to Syria
- Salmān sent word to the Christians:
  - If merchants come from Syria, inform me.
- When Syrian Christian merchants arrived:
  - He asked to be told when they were about to leave.
- When they prepared to return:
  - He threw off his fetters.
  - Fled with them.
  - Travelled until he reached Syria.

******* 5. Syria: the first bishop (corruption exposed)
- In Syria, Salmān asked:
  - Who is the best person in this religion?
- He was directed to the bishop.
- Salmān asked to stay, serve, learn, and pray with him:
  - The bishop accepted.
- Salmān discovered the bishop’s corruption:
  - He urged people to give charity.
  - He kept much of it for himself.
  - He amassed seven chests of gold and silver.
- When the bishop died:
  - Christians gathered to bury him.
  - Salmān exposed his hoarded treasure.
  - They refused to bury him.
  - They crucified him and pelted him with stones.

******* 6. Syria: a righteous worshipper and first referral
- Another man was appointed in the bishop’s place.
- Salmān described him as:
  - Exceptionally devoted.
  - Ascetic (shunned the world).
  - Seeking the Hereafter.
  - Striving night and day.
- Salmān loved him deeply and stayed with him.
- When he was dying, Salmān asked:
  - To whom should I go next?
- He replied:
  - He knew no one left upon what he followed,
  - Except a man in Mosul.
  - He instructed Salmān to go to him.

******* 7. Mosul → Nasībīn → ʿAmmūriyyah (a chain of teachers)
- Mosul:
  - Salmān went to the man there.
  - Found him righteous and upon the same way.
  - Soon he died.
  - He directed Salmān to a man in Nasībīn.
- Nasībīn:
  - Salmān went and stayed with the man there.
  - Found him upon the same way.
  - Soon he died.
  - He directed Salmān to a man in ʿAmmūriyyah.
- ʿAmmūriyyah:
  - Salmān stayed with him.
  - He worked and earned wealth.
  - He acquired cows and sheep.
  - Then that teacher’s death approached.

******* 8. Prophecy of the final Prophet (signs and location)
- When the teacher in ʿAmmūriyyah was dying, Salmān asked:
  - To whom should I go now?
- He replied:
  - He knew no one left upon their way to send him to,
  - But the time of a Prophet had come.
- He described the Prophet’s signs:
  - He will be sent with the religion of Ibrāhīm.
  - He will appear in the land of the Arabs.
  - He will migrate to a land:
    - Between two ḥarrah (black rocky/lava fields).
    - With palm trees between them.
  - He will eat gifts.
  - He will not eat charity (ṣadaqah).
  - Between his shoulder blades is the Seal of Prophethood.
- He instructed Salmān:
  - If you can reach that land, then do so.

******* 9. Journey toward Arabia and being sold as a slave
- After the teacher died:
  - Salmān remained in ʿAmmūriyyah for a time.
- Merchants from Kalb passed by:
  - Salmān offered his cows and sheep in exchange for taking him to the land of the Arabs.
  - They agreed.
- Betrayal at Wādī al-Qurā:
  - They wronged him.
  - Sold him as a slave to a Jewish man.
- He saw palm trees:
  - Hoped it matched the description,
  - But was not fully sure.
- Transfer to Madīnah:
  - The Jewish owner sold him to a cousin from Banū Qurayẓah in Madīnah.
  - Once Salmān saw Madīnah, he recognized it by the description he had been given.

******* 10. Hearing of the Prophet ﷺ arriving (Qubāʾ)
- Salmān remained busy with the work of slavery:
  - He did not hear much while the Messenger ﷺ was in Makkah.
- After the Hijrah:
  - Salmān was working atop a palm tree for his master.
  - A relative came saying:
    - People are gathering in Qubāʾ welcoming a man from Makkah.
    - They claim he is a Prophet.
- Salmān reacted strongly:
  - He trembled and feared falling.
  - He climbed down, questioning the man repeatedly.
- His master became angry:
  - Struck Salmān.
  - Told him to return to work.

******* 11. Testing the signs: charity (ṣadaqah)
- Salmān gathered what he had collected.

- Passage from salama hadith
  - The Messenger of Allah (ﷺ) sent his camels out with his slave, Rabah.
  - The narrator (Salama b. al-Akwa’) was with Rabah, and also went to the pasture with Talha’s horse alongside the camels.

  - At daybreak, Abd al-Rahman al-Fazari raided them:
    - He drove off *all* the camels of the Messenger of Allah (ﷺ).
    - He killed the man who was looking after the camels.

  - Salama instructed Rabah:
    - Ride Talha’s horse.
    - Go to Talha b. ‘Ubaidullah.
    - Inform the Messenger of Allah (ﷺ) that the polytheists have taken the camels.

  - Salama raised an alarm with a loud cry:
    - He stood on a hill, faced Medina, and shouted three times: “Come to our help!”

  - Salama pursued the raiders:
    - He shot at them with arrows.
    - He chanted :
      - “I am the son of al-Akwa’”
      - “Today is the day of defeat for the mean.”
    - He would overtake individuals, shoot an arrow that pierced the saddle and reached the shoulder, and say “Take it!”
    - He continued:
      - shooting at them,
      - hamstringing their animals.

  - If a horseman turned on him:
    - He would hide by a tree (sitting at its base),
    - then shoot and hamstring the horse.

  - The raiders entered a narrow mountain gorge:
    - Salama climbed up and held them back by throwing stones.
    - He kept chasing until:
      - all the Messenger’s camels were released,
      - and none remained with the raiders.

  - The raiders began dropping equipment to lighten their load:
    - More than 30 mantles.
    - 30 lances.
    - Salama marked each dropped item with a stone so the Messenger (ﷺ) and Companions would recognize it as enemy booty.

  - They reached a narrow valley and were joined by (so-and-so), son of Badr al-Fazari:
    - The raiders sat down for breakfast.
    - Salama sat on top of a tapering rock above them.

  - Al-Fazari asked who Salama was:
    - They replied that Salama had harassed them since dusk and kept shooting until he took everything.

  - Al-Fazari ordered four men to rush and kill Salama:
    - Four climbed toward him.
    - Salama said:
      - “Do you recognize me?”
      - “I am Salama, son of al-Akwa’.”
      - “By the One who honored the face of Muhammad (ﷺ), I can kill any of you I like, but none of you can kill me.”
    - One of them said he believed Salama was right.
    - They returned and did not attack.

  - Salama then saw cavalry from the Messenger of Allah (ﷺ) arriving through the trees:
    - The foremost was Akhram al-Asadi.
    - Behind him was Abu Qatada al-Ansari.
    - Behind him was al-Miqdad b. al-Aswad al-Kindi.

  - Salama caught Akhram’s rein as the raiders fled and warned him:
    - “Guard yourself until the Messenger of Allah (ﷺ) and his Companions join you.”
    - Akhram replied that if Salama truly believed in Allah and the Last Day (and Paradise and Hell), he should not block him from martyrdom.
    - So Salama let him go toward the enimies by camel, however salama rushed faster than the camels and was able to get there before.
      - he was the raider
  - Combat occurred:
    - Akhram fought Abd al-Rahman al-Fazari:
      - Akhram hamstrung Abd al-Rahman’s horse.
      - Abd al-Rahman struck Akhram with a lance and killed him.
      - Abd al-Rahman then rode off on Akhram’s horse.
        - Akram becanme a maytr
    - Abu Qatada fought Abd al-Rahman:
      - Abu Qatada struck him with his lance and killed him.

    - Salama continued the chase on foot with great speed:
      - He said he could not see the Companions behind him, nor the dust from their horses.

    - Before sunset, the raiders reached a valley with a spring called *Dhu Qarad* to drink (they were thirsty):
      - They saw Salama running toward them.

After the Conquest of Makkah
- The Prophet ﷺ remained in Makkah for about 19 days (reported from Ibn ʿAbbās, mentioned in the context of shortening prayers while staying in one place). 
- During this period, a number of people in Makkah came and pledged allegiance (bayʿah) to the Prophet ﷺ.
- Some people wanted to pledge specifically for Hijrah (migration to Madinah), but the Prophet ﷺ clarified that Hijrah from Makkah to Madinah ended after the Conquest of Makkah.
  - He still accepted pledges for Islam, and emphasized that jihad/striving and sincere intention remain. 

Battle of Ḥunayn (against Hawāzin/Thaqīf)
- Took place in the valley of Ḥunayn, between Makkah and Ṭāʾif, shortly after the Conquest of Makkah. 
- The Muslims heard that tribes around the area—especially Hawāzin and Thaqīf and their allies—were gathering to fight. 
- The Muslim army was about 12,000:
  - ~10,000 who came with the Prophet ﷺ (from Madinah) and
    - ~2,000 newly converted Quraysh (from Makkah). 

Incident of “Dhāt Anwāṭ” (the “tree” request)
- On the way to Ḥunayn, the Muslims passed a tree the polytheists called Dhāt Anwāṭ, where they used to hang weapons (seeking blessing).
- Some companions (newly away from jāhiliyyah) asked: “Make for us a Dhāt Anwāṭ like they have.”
  - The Prophet ﷺ strongly corrected them, comparing it to what the people of Mūsā said (Qur’an 7:138), warning against copying earlier nations in matters that resemble shirk.
