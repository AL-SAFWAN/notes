#+title: The book of Hajj
Ḥajj:
- linguistically: to intend.
- technically: to intend the sacred sites to complete the rituals.
- it is a right of Allāh that must be fulfilled once in their lifetime.
- it was made farḍ in the 9th year in Hijrah.
- he was unable to perform it in the first year:
  - it was the year of delegations.
  - there were 2 reasons:
    - delegations came and were invited to Islām,
    - and he told Abū Bakr to convey instructions to the people to clear the practices of Jāhiliyyah,
      - so that when the Prophet ﷺ performed Ḥajj it would be in the most perfect manner, which we can follow and not get confused by the practices of Jāhiliyyah.
- If you can do multiple Ḥajj, then do it.
  - Ḥajj increases your provision.
- Everyone is forgiven in Ḥajj.
  - it is the day that Allāh descends in a way that befits His Majesty.


- to leave Ḥajj intentionally:
  - is a major sin, but not kufr.

* 5 conditions for Ḥajj to be made farḍ on the person:
1. Islām.
2. bulūgh.
   - if the child does Ḥajj it is valid, but it does not count as the obligatory Ḥajj.
3. freedom from slavery.
   - it is still valid if they do it, but it is not farḍ on them to do it.
4. sanity.
5. istiṭā‘ah (the ability to perform it).
   - to possess the wealth and the mount:
     - enough wealth to go to Ḥajj, complete it, and return from it.
     - for the head of the household:
       - in addition to this: to leave enough wealth for your family when you go to Ḥajj, and enough money to return and continue with your life.
     - e.g. someone has a mount:
       - it is based on the custom of the people.
   - if a woman requires Ḥajj, she needs to be with a maḥram.
     - if she does not have a maḥram for Ḥajj:
       - then it is not farḍ for her.
       - if she goes without a maḥram:
         - her Ḥajj is still valid,
           - however she is sinful:
             - view of the Ḥanābilah and Ḥanafiyyah.
       - unless she finds a maḥram that is going to Ḥajj (e.g. her brother).
         - the husband has no right to prevent her.
         - there is no harm in the maḥram paying for her.
     - the maḥram is only for the journey to get to Makkah and to get back home.

* How to perform Ḥajj: the general framework
** enter the state of iḥrām:
- it is a religious state:
  - by making the intention you enter the state of iḥrām,
    - and you are called a muḥrim.
    - you make certain things that are ḥalāl become ḥarām for you.
    - it must be done before the mīqāt:
      - so it cannot be crossed until you are in the state of iḥrām.
- if you go without iḥrām:
  - you have 2 options:
    - assume iḥrām where you are,
      - and sacrifice an animal,
    - or go back to the mīqāt and assume iḥrām.

*** the boundary of the Ka‘bah is called the Ḥaram,
  - and outside the Ḥaram it is called Ḥill.
  - the Ḥaram is the sacred area of Makkah,
  - the Ḥill is the non-sacred area of Makkah,
  - after the Ḥill is the point of the mīqāt,
    - and after the mīqāt is the rest of the world.

*** When to enter the state of iḥrām
- (1) if you are outside the mīqāt, you must assume iḥrām.
- (2) if you live within the mīqāt and the Ḥaram:
  - you assume iḥrām from where you are.
- (3) if you live in the Ḥaram of Makkah:
  - you assume iḥrām from your house.
  - for ‘Umrah, you must leave the Ḥaram and go to the Ḥill and assume iḥrām there.
    - why?
      - you need to combine the Ḥill and Ḥaram for the rituals,
        - hence Masjid ‘Ā’ishah.

** ‘Umrah (summary)

When performing ‘Umrah:
1. iḥrām:
   - he intends ‘Umrah.
2. ṭawāf + 2 rak‘āt:
   - going around the Ka‘bah 7 times.
3. sa‘ī:
   1. Ṣafā to Marwah (x1),
   2. Marwah to Ṣafā (x1),
      - until 7 circuits are completed,
        - and the last will be Marwah.
4. shaving the head or trimming:
   - this completes your ‘Umrah and takes you out of the state of iḥrām.

** Ḥajj

- types of Ḥajj:
  - at-Tamattu‘: ‘Umrah and Ḥajj separately.
  - al-Qirān: ‘Umrah + Ḥajj as one action.
  - al-Ifrād: just Ḥajj, no ‘Umrah.

- two differences between Qirān and Ifrād:
  1. niyyah (just Ḥajj vs. Ḥajj + ‘Umrah),
  2. hady (the sacrificial animal).

- the best Ḥajj is at-Tamattu‘.
  - there is major khilāf:
    - the Prophet ﷺ instructed the ṣaḥābah to do this.

** Locations

- Ka‘bah,
- Jamārāt (the pillars) in Minā,
  - there are 3 pillars:
    - the biggest is the closest to Makkah:
      - Jamrat al-‘Aqabah.
      - today they are all the same size.
- Muzdalifah,
- ‘Arafah.

This is the order of where things are.

** Breakdown of Ḥajj
*** before the 8th
- enter Makkah before the Ḥajj days (e.g. 5th Dhul Ḥijjah):
  - the one doing at-Tamattu‘:
    - he will do and complete ‘Umrah,
      - and trim the hair if there is not much time between Ḥajj.
        - if bald, he can wipe over the head.
*** 8th Mina 
- then on the 8th:
  - he will enter into iḥrām and go to Minā,
    - from wherever he is.
  - from this point till the end of Ḥajj:
    - all of the 3 types of Ḥajj will be the same.
    - ‘Arafah is on the 9th and ‘Īd is on the 10th.

- the one doing Ifrād and Qirān:
  - case 1:
    - go straight to Minā on the 8th:
      - no ṭawāf or sa‘ī.
  - case 2:
    - perform ṭawāf when they enter Makkah:
      - sunnah ṭawāf,
      - if they miss the sunnah they can go straight to Minā.
      - just do ṭawāf + 2 rak‘āt.
  - case 3:
    - enter Makkah and:
      - do sunnah ṭawāf,
      - do the sa‘ī of Ḥajj:
        - and they do not need to do it again.
      - they do not cut the hair,

- then go to Minā on the 8th.

- it is sunnah to go to Minā (the tents) midday.
- in Minā:
  - they pray Ẓuhr till ‘Ishā’:
    - shortened, not combined.
*** 9th Mina -> Arafat -> Muzdalifah
- still in Minā:
  - they pray Fajr in Minā on the 9th day,
    - then go to ‘Arafah.
      - they pass by Muzdalifah.
      - if you have the option to walk or go by bus:
        - choose the easiest.

- in ‘Arafah:
  - you pray Ẓuhr and ‘Aṣr:
    - shortened and combined.
  - you make du‘ā’ until Maghrib.
    - the whole of ‘Arafah is a place of standing:
      - you can do it sitting,
        - until Maghrib.
  - you go from ‘Arafah to Muzdalifah after Maghrib:
    - this is considered the 10th night.

- in Muzdalifah:
  - you pray Maghrib and ‘Ishā’:
    - shortened and combined.
  - pray Witr,
  - then go to sleep for the night,
*** 10th  Muzdalifah -> Jamrat (Mina) -> Makkah -> Mina
- still in Muzdalifah:
  - then wake up on the 10th morning:
    - you pray Fajr in Muzdalifah,
      - the 10th day is also the day of ‘Īd:
        - Yawm an-Naḥr,
          - the day of sacrifice.

The ‘ulamā’ mention: “ranḥat”:
- ra = rāmī (to throw),
- n = naḥr (to sacrifice),
- ḥa = ḥalq (to shave),
- ta = ṭawāf.

- we make du‘ā’ just before sunrise,
  - then leave Muzdalifah,
    - and complete the ranḥat.

- we go to Jamrat al-‘Aqabah (the 3rd pillar):
  - it is the closest to Makkah and the furthest to Minā,
    - passing the 1st and 2nd pillars.
  - 7 pebbles:
    - you throw overhand,
      - and say “Allāhu Akbar.”
    - if it does not hit the pillar then it is OK:
      - since it will drop down and actually hit the real pillar.

- then the one doing at-Tamattu‘ and Qirān:
  - will sacrifice the animal.
  - after sacrificing the animal:
    - you shave or trim the hair,
      - then you do the ṭawāf (7 circuits) of Ḥajj + 2 rak‘āt:
        - this is ṭawāf al-ifāḍah of Ḥajj.

- then to sa‘ī (Ṣafā to Marwah):
  - Tamattu‘:
    - must do the sa‘ī of Ḥajj.
  - Ifrād and Qirān:
    - if he already did the sa‘ī of Ḥajj, then he does not do sa‘ī again.
    - if he did not do the sa‘ī of Ḥajj, then he will do sa‘ī.
      
Mas’alah:
- ranḥat is the sunnah order:
  - the view of the majority:
    - you can do it in any order.

- the “green one” is not for everyone in Ḥajj:
  - only for Tamattu‘ and Qirān.

- ra-ḥa-ta:
  - if you do 2 of the 3:
    - you come out of the first stage of iḥrām,
      - which means everything that is ḥarām becomes ḥalāl except marital relations.
        - e.g. using perfume,
        - you can take the garments of iḥrām off.
- when you complete the rest of this:
  - after ṭawāf and sa‘ī:
    - you are no longer in iḥrām on the 10th.

*** 11th Jamrat (Mina)
- on the 11th:
  - we stay in Minā.
  - after Ẓuhr:
    - we pray in Minā:
      - shortened and not combined.
    - we throw 7 stones at the 1st pillar,
      - and make du‘ā’,
    - then 7 stones at the 2nd pillar,
      - and make du‘ā’,
    - then 7 stones at the 3rd pillar,
      - and make du‘ā’.
    - 21 stones in total.
  - this is after we have been to every site.
*** 12th Jamrat (Mina)
- on the 12th:
  - same as the 11th.
  - now there is an option (for all 3 types):
    - case 1:
      - stay in Minā:
        - if you stay for Maghrib, you stay for the night.
    - case 2:
      - leave Minā before Maghrib for the 13th.
*** 13th Jamrat (Mina)
- on the 13th (optional):
  - same as the 11th (if you stay),
    - and you must complete it before Maghrib.
      
*** farwell tawaf Makkah
- the only thing left is the farewell ṭawāf:
  - this is farḍ for everyone except for the woman on her menses.

** There are 6 emphasised times to make du‘ā’:
1. Mount Ṣafā.
2. Mount Marwah.
3. ‘Arafah until Maghrib.
4. Muzdalifah:
   - after praying Fajr and before sunrise.
5. After throwing the stones at the 1st pillar (11–13):
   - length of al-Baqarah.
6. After the 2nd pillar (11–13):
   - length of Āl ‘Imrān.



* Foundations: obligation and ability (=istiṭā‘ah=)

- *269.* Evidence for the obligation of =Ḥajj= comes from the Qur’ān (3:97): it is a duty owed to Allah for *those who are able to undertake it*.
- *270.* The greatest condition for =Ḥajj= is *ability to go* (=istiṭā‘ah=), meaning:
  - having the provisions and means of transport needed for the journey, *and*
  - having one’s basic living needs covered.
- *271.* For a woman, being accompanied by a =maḥram= (close male relative) is included in =istiṭā‘ah= *if she needs to travel*.

* Core reference: ḥadīth of Jābir (Muslim) — the Prophet’s Ḥajj ﷺ

- *272.* The ḥadīth of Jābir رضي الله عنه regarding the Ḥajj of the Prophet ﷺ contains the most important rulings of =Ḥajj=.
** Condensed timeline (key points)

- The Prophet ﷺ announced his intention to perform =Ḥajj= in the *tenth year*, and many people came to follow him.
- At the =mīqāt= of =Dhūl-Ḥulayfah=:
  - Asmāʾ bint ʿUmays gave birth and asked what to do.
  - He ﷺ instructed: *take a bath*, bandage the private area, and *enter =iḥrām=*.
- He ﷺ pronounced the oneness of Allah and recited the =talbiyah=.
  - People recited talbiyah in different wordings; he ﷺ did not reject them, and he ﷺ kept to his own talbiyah.
- Upon entering Makkah:
  - He ﷺ touched the Black Stone and performed *seven circuits* around the Kaʿbah.
  - He ﷺ jogged lightly in the first *three* circuits and walked the remaining *four*.
  - He ﷺ prayed *two rakʿahs* near the Station of Ibrāhīm and then returned to the Black Stone.
  - He ﷺ went out towards =Ṣafā= and began =saʿī= between =Ṣafā= and =Marwah=.
- At the end of =saʿī=, he ﷺ instructed those who *did not have sacrificial animals* to leave =iḥrām= and treat it as an =ʿUmrah=.
  - This was clarified as a ruling for the community “forever and ever.”
- ‘Alī (رضي الله عنه) arrived with sacrificial animals and found Fāṭimah (رضي الله عنها) had left =iḥrām=; the Prophet ﷺ affirmed her action.
  - The total number of sacrificial animals mentioned was *one hundred*.
  - Everyone *except* the Prophet ﷺ and those who had sacrificial animals left =iḥrām= and clipped their hair.
- On the Day of =Tarwiyah= (8th of Dhūl-Ḥijjah):
  - the pilgrims went to =Minā= and entered =iḥrām= for =Ḥajj=.
  - the Prophet ﷺ led the prayers there (Ẓuhr, ʿAṣr, Maghrib, ʿIshāʾ, and Fajr).
- At =ʿArafah=:
  - He ﷺ delivered a sermon, emphasising:
    - the sanctity of blood and property,
    - the abolishment of pre-Islamic practices (including blood-revenge and usury),
    - and good conduct regarding women,
    - and holding fast to the Book of Allah.
  - He ﷺ led =Ẓuhr= and =ʿAṣr= together, with no prayer between them.
  - He ﷺ stood at the place of standing, facing the =qiblah=, until sunset.
- At =Muzdalifah=:
  - He ﷺ travelled with calmness and moderation.
  - He ﷺ combined =Maghrib= and =ʿIshāʾ= (one adhān and two iqāmahs), then slept until dawn.
  - He ﷺ prayed =Fajr= when the morning light was clear, then made dhikr at =al-Mashʿar al-Ḥarām=, and departed before sunrise.
- On the Day of Sacrifice (10th), at =Minā=:
  - He ﷺ stoned the greatest =jamrah= with *seven* small pebbles, saying *Allāhu Akbar* with each throw.
  - He ﷺ sacrificed *sixty-three* (camels) with his own hand, then gave the remainder to ‘Alī رضي الله عنه to sacrifice.
  - A piece of meat from each animal was cooked; the Prophet ﷺ and ‘Alī ate from it and drank its broth.
  - He ﷺ then went to Makkah, prayed =Ẓuhr=, and drank from Zamzam.

** Ḥadīth of Jābir
- consists of the majority of the rulings.
  - mention the main ḥadīth when starting the chapter.
  - the most comprehensive ḥadīth related to Ḥajj.
- the Prophet ﷺ died a year after it.

- Dhul Ḥulayfah:
  - is the mīqāt for the people of Madīnah.
  - the furthest mīqāt from Makkah:
    - going from the ‘ibādah of Madīnah to the ‘ibādah of Makkah.
  - Asmā’ bint ‘Umays:
    - delivered a child.
      - what can she do?
        - perform ghusl or remove the najāsah,
        - and bandage the private part so najāsah does not spread.
  - women on their menses:
    - must still assume iḥrām from the mīqāt.
      - Masjid ‘Ā’ishah is not a mīqāt:
        - it is a Ḥill.
  - there are also other mawāqīt.

- he ﷺ prayed in Dhul Ḥulayfah:
  - and then he prayed a farḍ prayer.
    - it is sunnah to pray first and then enter the state of iḥrām.

- then he mounted the animal called al-Qaṣwā’.
- until the place of al-Bayḍā’ (a place near the mīqāt),
  - then proclaimed tawḥīd:
    - the people entered the iḥrām of the Prophet ﷺ.

- the different types of Ḥajj were not known:
  - the intention of which type of Ḥajj is not emphasised,
    - compared to the intention for iḥrām.
  - you can intend the Ḥajj of the person you are following.
  - for the one who is performing ‘Umrah.

- he stops at the Ka‘bah / the Ḥaram:
  - the one who is doing Ḥajj until the pillars will continue until the 10th.

- you can greet the corner of the Ka‘bah:
  - by kissing it and saying: bismillāh, Allāhu akbar,
  - or touch it without kissing,
  - or raise your hand and greet it.

- then rush around the Ka‘bah 3 times:
  - for men only.
  - the right shoulder is uncovered for men.
  - you can see the Ka‘bah and make du‘ā’:
    - there is nothing authentic about a specific du‘ā’ for this,
      - and it is not authentically reported from the ṣaḥābah.
  - then they walk the other 4 times.
    - this is called ramal:
      - this is only done with iḥrām,
      - and only for the first ṭawāf.
        - for the other ṭawāf:
          - you do it without ramal.
  - if you have women in your family, you will not do this.

- face the Ka‘bah if you cannot kiss it:
  - even if you do not say anything during ṭawāf:
    - it does not take reward away from it.
  - ṭawāf is like ṣalāh except you can speak in it.

- at the time of the Prophet ﷺ it was on a foundation that was originally built upon:
  - then after the flood:
    - they used only ḥalāl wealth to rebuild the Ka‘bah.
      - hence it was rebuilt (and they did not complete the full foundation),
        - because they did not have enough wealth.
  - the semi-circle (Ḥijr / Ḥaṭīm) indicates the part that is outside the current structure.
  - only two sides are upon the original foundation of Ibrāhīm:
    - the corner of the Black Stone and the Yemeni corner:
      - hence it is sunnah to touch.
  - the first side is the side of the door,
  - the second side is the side of the Ḥijr,
  - the fourth side is the Yemeni corner:
    - you just wipe it with your hand,
      - and nothing else.
    - do not treat it like the Black Stone.
    - if you cannot wipe it, then treat it like it is not there.

- when you get to the Stone, 1 circuit is complete:
  - turn to the Stone, raise your hand,
    - and you can say only: Allāhu akbar (a sunnah).
    - there is no harm to say: bismillāh, Allāhu akbar,
      - as it has been authentically reported from the ṣaḥābah.
  - then 2nd–7th time are the same.
  - you do not need to go to the Stone again:
    - it is only used to start the ṭawāf, not finish it.

- then (pray) at Maqām Ibrāhīm:
  - there is an āyah about it:
    - “take the station of Ibrāhīm as a place of prayer”.
      - is it sunnah to say a specific verse as part of the ritual, or is it part of teaching the ritual?
  - the stone of Ibrāhīm was right next to the Ka‘bah:
    - then due to the crowds:
      - ‘Umar moved it to where it is today,
        - and it was approved by the ṣaḥābah.
  - it is permissible to pray anywhere in the masjid:
    - even if you pray outside the masjid it is permissible,
    - anywhere in the Ḥaram of Makkah.
  - he ﷺ recited Sūrah al-Ikhlāṣ and Sūrah al-Kāfirūn.

- then he went back to the Black Stone and greeted it:
  - the ‘ulamā’ say it is only if you can kiss it or touch it.
    - this also shows the permissibility to kiss or touch it even if you are not doing ṭawāf.

- drinking Zamzam at this point:
  - a person can drink it any time,
    - and it is not part of the ritual.
  - there is nothing authentically reported to make du‘ā’ after the 2 rak‘āt.
  - there is nothing authentic reporting a specific du‘ā’ after the 2 rak‘āt.

- when he went to Ṣafā:
  - you recite only part of the āyah of Ṣafā:
    - “Indeed, Ṣafā and Marwah are among the symbols of Allāh…”
      - they differ on this:
        - to show if it is a ritual or to teach.
  - the tiles are part of Mount Ṣafā.
  - ascend Mount Ṣafā until he ﷺ faced the qiblah and made takbīr, then made du‘ā’:
    - then you make your own du‘ā’.
    - you do this 3 times:
      - the sunnah is:
        - he faced the qiblah,
        - then said the remembrance,
        - then made his own du‘ā’,
        - 3 times.

- descend the mountain to Marwah:
  - until the feet touched the bottom of the valley and he ﷺ rushed:
    - sunnah for men to run:
      - as fast as you can.
  - then he would walk until he reached Marwah:
    - you do everything the same as Ṣafā,
      - but it does not include saying the āyah.
  - until the last of the circuits ends at Marwah.

- if the person is doing at-Tamattu‘ and ‘Umrah:
  - he only has his hair left to cut.
  - he ﷺ brought his sacrificial animal from Madīnah.
    - when the āyah came down, he ﷺ said: if he knew then what he knew now, he would not have brought the sacrificial animal.
      - whoever came without it:
        - turn it to an ‘Umrah:
          - meaning: at-Tamattu‘.
        - hence the intention can be changed from Qirān to at-Tamattu‘.
          - since they started with Ḥajj with the Prophet ﷺ:
            - ‘Umrah entered into Ḥajj.
      - so if you came with it:
        - you cannot perform at-Tamattu‘.
          - this means he ﷺ did Qirān at the time, and most did at-Tamattu‘.
            - hence the ikhtilāf of which is the best Ḥajj.

- for Qirān and Ifrād:
  - he has done this:
    - it means he did the ṭawāf for entering,
    - and the sa‘ī for Ḥajj,
    - until the 8th.

- after du‘ā’ of sa‘ī:
  - it is to start the round, not to end it.

*** 8th day of Dhul Ḥijjah
- then went to Minā with their mount:
  - and entered iḥrām for Ḥajj.
- in Minā:
  - prayed Ẓuhr to ‘Ishā’:
    - shortened and not combined.
- he asked for a tent made out of fur:
  - now a masjid is made there called Masjid Namirah.
    - this is in the boundary of ‘Arafah:
      - they differ if it was part of it or not:
        - the front part is not from ‘Arafah.
      - Ḥajj is ‘Arafah.

- then from there he ﷺ went towards al-Mash‘ar al-Ḥarām to ‘Arafah:
  - there was a tent place for him in Namirah.
  - he ﷺ asked for his camel called al-Qaṣwā’:
    - then went to Wādī ‘Uranah:
      - and gave the khuṭbah.
      - the practices of Jāhiliyyah:
        - all of it has been abolished.

- every person who saw and heard from the Prophet ﷺ:
  - who became a Muslim and died with īmān:
    - this person is a ṣaḥābī:
      - they are the best of people, from those who came after.
      - the virtue of being with the Prophet ﷺ increased their rank.
      - the longer you meet the Prophet ﷺ, the more you increase in rank and virtue.
      - you love every companion without exception:
        - refrain from their conflicts and errors.

- he prayed Ẓuhr and ‘Aṣr:
  - combined and shortened,
  - in ‘Arafah.
- as a traveller, he ﷺ prayed the 2 from Fajr only.
- all of ‘Arafah is a place of du‘ā’.
- he ﷺ was on his mount making du‘ā’ till sunset.

- what was the need for combining?
  - to give more time to make du‘ā’.
- the red and yellow of the sun until it had set.

*** After ‘Arafah
- then he went to Muzdalifah:
  - to pray Maghrib and ‘Ishā’.
    - why combine?
      - so they can rest for the night.
    - if you want to pray Witr, you can.
  - then pray Fajr:
    - then made du‘ā’ till just before sunrise.
    - then leave to Wādī Muḥaṣṣir:
      - he rushed,
      - because this is where the ‘adhāb of the people of the elephant took place.
        - they even avoided taking the water from there.

- he ﷺ went to the Jamrah (Jamrat al-‘Aqabah) and threw 7 pebbles:
  - size of a chickpea.
  - threw from the valley.

- then he went to the slaughter place:
  - difference between dhabḥ (smaller animals) and naḥr (done with camels; can be done with cows).
  - he ﷺ did 63 and ‘Alī did the rest.
  - they ate from the meat and drank from the broth:
    - the meat is sunnah to eat,
      - but the meat for compensation: you cannot eat from it.

- then he ﷺ went to the Ka‘bah:
  - and drank Zamzam at the same time.

* Taking the Prophet’s rites ﷺ

- *273.* The Prophet ﷺ said: *“Take from me your rites.”*
  - The most complete pilgrimage is to follow the Prophet ﷺ and his Companions.
** Principles
- performing more actions is not more virtuous:
  - performing the sunnah is more virtuous than more actions.
- likewise in the days of Tashrīq:
  - it is to stay in Minā:
    - so take the guidance of the Prophet ﷺ.
      - the most complete Ḥajj is the Ḥajj of the Prophet ﷺ.

- the ṣaḥābah, just by looking at the Prophet ﷺ, became ‘ulamā’:
  - but for us, we need to go through many sciences,
    - and we still do not know if it is right or wrong.

The ḥadīth above is not the complete ritual of Ḥajj.

If the one performing Ḥajj limited himself to the 4 pillars and the wājibāt, it will be sufficient for him, and his Ḥajj will be valid.

* What suffices for a valid Ḥajj: pillars and obligations

- *274.* If a pilgrim restricts themselves to the following, it suffices:

*** The four *pillars* (=arkān=)
1. iḥrām by ijmā‘.
2. standing in ‘Arafāt by ijmā‘.
3. ṭawāf by ijmā‘.
4. sa‘ī according to the majority.

*** The *obligations* (=wājibāt=)

1. Enter =iḥrām= from the designated point (=mīqāt=)
2. Stay in =ʿArafah= until sunset
3. Stay overnight in =Muzdalifah= (the night before the sacrifice)
4. Spend the days of =Tashrīq= in =Minā=
5. Throw the stones at the =Jamārāt=
6. Shave or shorten one’s hair
7. the farewell ṭawāf.

- *275.* Difference between leaving a *pillar* (=rukn=) vs leaving an *obligation* (=wājib=):
  - Leaving a *pillar*: the =Ḥajj= is not accepted until the pillar is performed in the prescribed manner.
  - Leaving an *obligation*: the =Ḥajj= remains correct, but the person is sinful and must offer a sacrifice for leaving it out.
    
The pillar must be performed; there is no expiation for it. For wājibāt, you can expiate.
- the difference is:
  - the one who leaves the pillar: the Ḥajj will not be valid until he performs it.
  - if you leave the wājib:
    - you are sinful and need to give a sacrifice.

Leaving an obligation necessitates a sacrifice.
Doing a prohibition is not the same:
- i.e. doing a prohibition of iḥrām.

* Types of Ḥajj

- *276.* When entering =iḥrām= one may perform:
  - =tamattuʿ= (*best*),
  - or =qirān=,
  - or =ifrād=.
- *277.* =Tamattuʿ=: enter =iḥrām= intending =ʿUmrah= in the months of =Ḥajj=, complete it and exit =iḥrām=, then re-enter =iḥrām= for =Ḥajj= in the same year.
- *278.* =Ifrād=: enter =iḥrām= for =Ḥajj= only.
- *279.* =Qirān=: enter =iḥrām= for both (=ʿUmrah= and =Ḥajj=), or enter =iḥrām= for =ʿUmrah= then include =Ḥajj= in it before beginning =ṭawāf=.
- *280.* A person intending =tamattuʿ= shifts to the method of =qirān= when:
  1. they fear missing the standing at =ʿArafah= due to being preoccupied with =ʿUmrah=, or
  2. a woman menstruates or has post-natal bleeding and knows she will not become pure before the time of standing at =ʿArafah=.
- *281.* The actions of the =mufrid= and the =qārin= are the same, *but* the =qārin= must offer a sacrifice, whereas the =mufrid= does not.

At-Tamattu‘
- to go into the iḥrām of ‘Umrah in the months of Ḥajj:
  - Shawwāl, Dhul Qa‘dah, and Dhul Ḥijjah,
  - and then to complete it,
    - then to enter into the iḥrām of Ḥajj,
      - in the same year.
- the ‘Umrah for at-Tamattu‘, and going into the state of iḥrām for Ḥajj:
  - can happen between Shawwāl and the days of Ḥajj.
  - then he has to perform the Ḥajj in the same year:
    - if another year, he needs to do another ‘Umrah.
- he sacrifices an animal (the hady):
  - since he did ‘Umrah and Ḥajj separately:
    - except for those who live in al-Masjid al-Ḥarām.
- if you cannot (sacrifice):
  - then you fast on the days of Tashrīq.

Ifrād (called a mufrid):
- to perform Ḥajj by itself.

Qirān (called a qārin):
- to do the iḥrām with Ḥajj and ‘Umrah together.
- he enters to do ‘Umrah and changes for Ḥajj.

The one doing ‘Umrah can convert his ‘Umrah before his ṭawāf.

The one who is doing at-Tamattu‘ needs to change it from ‘Umrah to Ḥajj in 2 cases (from at-Tamattu‘ to Qirān):
1. if he does ‘Umrah late for at-Tamattu‘ and he might miss ‘Arafāt (a pillar):
   - as the one doing Qirān and Ifrād: the first wājib is on the day of ‘Arafāt, and this is a pillar.
   - if you miss ‘Arafāt, you miss Ḥajj.
   - the one coming for at-Tamattu‘:
     - what is farḍ for him is the ‘Umrah,
       - then the next obligation is ‘Arafāt after entering iḥrām.
     - hence if he fears he will miss ‘Arafāt he can change it to Ifrād.
       - e.g. a person might come on the 9th and fear missing ‘Arafāt:
         - this ‘Umrah might prevent him,
           - so he changes the intention.
2. if a woman begins her menses or post-natal bleeding and knows she will not be pure before ‘Arafāt:
   - e.g. enters on the 1st and is in her cycle till the 9th day:
     - she cannot perform the ‘Umrah,
       - so she needs to change her intention.
     - this happened to ‘Ā’ishah:
       - hence she went to the Ḥill and assumed iḥrām to perform ‘Umrah.

The qārin needs to do the sacrifice.
* =Iḥrām=: prohibitions and expiations

- *282.* The =muḥrim= must avoid:
maḥẓūrāt al-iḥrām
1. to shave or remove hair:
   - if by accident then there is no harm in it,
     - similar principle to fasting,
     - hair from anywhere of the body.
2. to clip the nails.
3. to wear makhīṭ (fitted garments):
   - e.g. shirt, thawb.
   - what is intended by this:
     - the garment that fits around the limb, not the stitching itself.
     - it is not the material but the way you wear the garment.
   - a man can wear two pieces of cloth; there is narration that he wore green:
     - it is sunnah for them to be white.
   - cannot wear socks or shoes:
     - you can wear something that is open at the front,
       - and has gaps where you can see the feet,
       - and the ankles are not covered,
       - and the strap is low.
     - e.g. Crocs — better to stay away from it.
   - Ḥanafī:
     - prohibited is that which covers the knuckles of the foot:
       - covering the protruding bone is prohibited,
         - meaning the ankle.
     - hence they only wear slippers.

   - for women:
     - the prohibitions are:
       - face covering:
         - do not do niqāb:
           - to pierce the holes.
         - there is no prohibition for a cloth to touch the face:
           - some women wear a hat and use that to drop it down on the face.
       - hand covering:
         - gloves, but can have extended sleeves.
4. to cover the head:
   - that which covers it directly like a hat,
     - not an umbrella or the roof of the car.
   - if sleeping you can cover the face with a blanket.
5. perfume:
   - for men and women.
   - for men:
     - you cannot put it on the garment,
       - but you can put it on your face and beard before iḥrām.
     - if the garment touches your body and starts to smell:
       - there is no harm in this.
6. to hunt:
   - animals of the land:
     - not the sea.
   - a wild animal:
     - not a domestic animal.
   - from the animals you can eat:
     - not a predator.
   - you cannot even point it out:
     - or assist in killing it.
7. ‘aqd an-nikāḥ:
   - to get married:
     - cannot propose, marry, or marry someone off.
8. touching the spouse with desire.
9. the severe prohibition is marital relations:
   - it spoils the entire Ḥajj:
     - and requires a camel.
   - must do:
     - tawbah,
     - complete the Ḥajj even though it is spoiled,
     - sacrifice a camel,
     - come back for Ḥajj the following year.


- *283.* =Fidyah al-adhā= (expiation for violating =iḥrām=) applies in cases such as:
  - a man covering his head or wearing tailored clothing, or
  - a woman covering her face, wearing gloves, or using perfume.
  The person chooses *one* of:
  1. fast three days
  2. feed six poor people
  3. slaughter a sheep

- *284.* If a person kills game, the options are:
  1. sacrifice an animal similar to the one killed
  2. estimate the value of the killed game and buy food to feed the poor with that amount
     - giving each person a handful of wheat or half a sāʿ of something else
  3. if unable to feed the poor, fast a day for each poor person that would have been fed

- *285.* The sacrificial animals of =tamattuʿ= and =qirān= must meet the conditions of the animal offered for =uḍḥiyah=.
- *286.* If one cannot find the required sacrificial animal, then they fast *ten days*:
  - three during =Ḥajj= (and it is permissible to fast them in the days of =Tashrīq=),
  - and seven after returning home.
- *287.* The same ruling applies when:
  1. a person leaves out an obligation, or
  2. a person is required to pay the fidyah due to intercourse.
- *288.* Any sacrifice or food given as expiation related to the =ḥaram= precinct or =iḥrām= is given to *the poor of the ḥaram* (whether residents or visitors).
- *289.* The fasting (as an expiation) can be done anywhere.
- *290.* It is recommended to eat from the sacrificial offering for rites such as =tamattuʿ=, =qirān=, and =hady=, and also to give some as a gift and as charity.
- *291.* It is not permitted to eat from an obligatory sacrifice that resulted from committing a prohibited act (=maḥẓūr=) in =iḥrām= or leaving out an obligation.
  - This is called =dam jabrān=.
  - It must be given away entirely as charity, as it is treated like an expiation (=kaffārah=).
Fidyat al-adhā
- name of the compensation that is given due to the prohibitions of iḥrām.
- has the choice of any one of the 3:
  - fast for 3 days,
  - feed 6 poor people:
    - 1 mudd of wheat, or half ṣā‘ of other than it,
  - sacrifice an animal.

However, for hunting:
- sacrifice an animal that resembles what you hunted from the livestock,
  - or give money equal to its value,
    - or fast for the amount of meals that could be fed by it:
      - fast according to what it could feed people with.
- if it does not resemble an animal:
  - give the money value of the animal,
  - or fast for each day that people can eat from the animal.

And nikāḥ:
- not valid.
  - tawbah is required,
    - no specific expiation.

To touch the spouse:
- only tawbah is required.

And marital relations:
- tawbah,
  - camel,
    - and Ḥajj in the following year.

That which is sufficient for uḍḥiyah is what you give for fidyat al-adhā.

- if you cannot find an animal:
  - you can fast 10 days:
    - 3 days in Ḥajj (days of Tashrīq) and 7 days when you come back.
  - likewise the one who leaves a wājib act:
    - however there is no evidence for this.
    - if they cannot find an animal:
      - then you should make tawbah:
        - you cannot make qiyās from one to the other.

- any type of sacrifice for iḥrām or for the Ḥaram:
  - you feed the people of the Ḥaram.
  - the fasting can be done anywhere.
  - the animal sacrifice for the ritual:
    - it is sunnah to eat from it, gift it, and give it in charity.
  - the farḍ sacrifice (when he does something prohibited or leaves the wājib):
    - he gives the sacrifice to make up the loss:
      - hence he cannot eat from it,
        - and it must be all for ṣadaqah.

* =Ṭawāf= and =Saʿī=

- *292.* Prerequisites of =ṭawāf=:
  1. intention
  2. begin from the Black Stone
     - touching or kissing it is recommended
     - if unable, gesture towards it with the hand
  3. keep the Kaʿbah to one’s left
  4. complete seven circuits
  5. be free from ritual and physical impurities

- *293.* Purification is *recommended* (not obligatory) for rites other than =ṭawāf=.
  - (The meaning conveyed): =ṭawāf= of the House is like prayer, except speech is permitted during it.

- *294.* Recommended in the *welcoming* =ṭawāf=:
  1. do =iḍṭibāʿ= (upper garment under the right armpit; garment over the left shoulder)
  2. lightly jog in the first three circuits and walk the rest
- *295.* It is not recommended to jog or do =iḍṭibāʿ= in other types of =ṭawāf=.
- *296.* Prerequisites of =saʿī=:
  1. intention
  2. complete seven laps
  3. start from =Ṣafā=
- *297.* One should remember Allah and call upon Him during =ṭawāf=, =saʿī=, and the other rites:
  - these rites are for establishing the remembrance of Allah.
    
it is sunnah to greet and kiss the Stone:
  - the ṭawāf is still valid without it.
- “Allāhu akbar”:
  - is what is authentically reported from the Prophet ﷺ,
    - and the rest via Ibn ‘Umar.

the Ka‘bah must be on your left side:
  - you must do it as 7 circuits.
  - you must not have najāsah and must have wuḍū’:
    - this is a condition of ṭawāf by the majority.
      - some say it is sunnah, but it is not encouraged to do ṭawāf without wuḍū’.
      - if they break wuḍū’ and it is crowded and causes great difficulty and time:
        - then they should still perform ṭawāf,
          - then make wuḍū’ after and pray the 2 rak‘āt.
    - another condition is to cover the ‘awrah.
  - as for having wuḍū’ in other rituals:
    - it is sunnah and not farḍ.
    - the woman in menses can do everything other than the ṭawāf.
    - she can pray the 2 rak‘āt of Maqām Ibrāhīm later on.

sunnah for men to uncover the right shoulder:
  - rush for the 3 circuits,
  - then normal for the rest,
    - but this is not for every ṭawāf.

- conditions of sa‘ī:
  - to have niyyah of sa‘ī,
  - complete the 7 circuits.

* The sanctity of Makkah and Madīnah
- Allāh prevented the elephants from destroying it,
  - and made it ḥarām to spill blood in Makkah,
    - and permitted it for the Prophet ﷺ for the conquest,
      - and not allowed for anyone after.
- Makkah has specific rules:
  - you cannot pick up something lost (luqaṭah),
  - scare the animals away,
  - remove trees and plants,
    - except al-idhkhir,
      - as this is sacred land.

- the Ḥaram of Madīnah:
  - is not as strict as Makkah.

- *298.* The sanctuary (=ḥaram=) of Makkah has sacred rulings:
  - protection of its sanctity,
  - prohibition of hunting game there and removing its vegetation/thorns,
  - lost property is not taken except by one who publicly announces it,
  - and an exception is mentioned for =idhkhir= (a type of herbage).
  - and in cases of killing: the family may have options (blood-money or just retribution).
- *299.* Madīnah is sacred between =ʿAyr= and =Thawr=.
- *300.* Five harmful creatures may be killed whether in =iḥrām= or not:
  - crow, kite, scorpion, mouse, and rabid dog.

* Chapter: the Hady, Uḍḥiyah & ʿAqīqah

- *301.* The obligatory sacrifice (=hady=) has already been mentioned for =tamattuʿ= and =qirān=. Other ritual sacrifices are *recommended* (e.g., =uḍḥiyah= and =ʿaqīqah=).
- *302.* Acceptable animals include:
  - a half-year-old lamb, and
  - =ath-thaniy=:
    - camels: five years old
    - cows: two years old
    - goats: one year old
- *303.* Four animals are not acceptable for sacrifice:
  1. one-eyed (clearly lost sight of one eye)
  2. clearly sick
  3. clearly lame
  4. an animal with a broken leg with no marrow
- *304.* The animal should be in a good state and free from defects; the more perfect it is, the more beloved to Allah and the more rewarding.
- *305.* A camel suffices for seven people, and a cow suffices for seven people.
- *306.* It is recommended for the father to perform the =ʿaqīqah=.
- *307.* For =ʿaqīqah=: two sheep for a boy, and one sheep for a girl.
- *308.* The =ʿaqīqah= is connected to the newborn:
  - slaughtering is done on the *seventh day*,
  - the child is named,
  - and the head is shaved.
- *309.* The slaughtered meat is distributed:
  - some is eaten,
  - some is given as a gift,
  - and some is given as charity.
- *310.* The slaughterer is not paid with meat; instead, give a gift or charity separately.
